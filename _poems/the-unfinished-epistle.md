---
title: The Unfinished Epistle
subtitle: or A Long Ending in Couplets
date: 
---

Still burns my breast when midnight bells are told,
And passion wrests the reins from reason's hold;
Not cloister'd nuns more anxiously would pray,
Than I, who kneel, yet teach my heart to stray.
Your name returns where vows forbid its sound,
And scented notes like censers smoke around.
O you, whose mind commands the stubborn stars,
Whose restless will makes peace a field of wars,
Accept these lines—unblest, unshriven, true—
A trembling soul pours all her storms to you.

Once pride was mine, to rule my little sphere,
To charm a room and hold a rival's fear;
Now pride is dust that clings to bleeding feet—
I kiss the dust where mind and ardour meet.
Your voice, at Oxford, broke my studied grace;
I held a court, and yet became your chase.
The Union's lamps, the jests, the thunder'd votes,
Fell dull and far when trembled all my notes.
What throne of wit could keep me safely crown'd,
When eyes like yours made every altar drown'd?

I loved your iron soul, your scholar's fire,
The cruel candour yoked to vast desire;
I loved you railing at the world's pretence,
I loved your mercy masking as offence.
I loved your charts that promised newer skies,
Your fierce contempt for smaller men's replies;
I loved your plans that made the future bow,
Then left me present, faithless to my vow.
And when our quarrels flared like molten steel,
I clung the more—alas, I learned to kneel.

Did I not swear, in jealous rage and tears,
To part, to purge, to silence lawless fears?
"Go, go"—I cried, then summon'd back your tread,
And crown'd with kisses every word I said.
What martyr bleeds with half my willing art,
Who nails herself and calls it wisdom's part?
One day I banish'd you with righteous zeal,
The next, I bid the banishment to kneel.
How strict my rule until I scent your name;
Then rules turn wax and melt in candle-flame.

Was it a sin to wish the world to burn,
If ash and dawn would see your swift return?
To watch the cab, to count the lights below,
To bar the door against a patient foe?
O Reason, pale preceptor, stoic mate,
Why did you come so late, and come so late?
You preach me peace where every pulse is storm,
You prune the rose and spare me but the thorn.
Yet still I listen, half-convinced by care,
And tie my heart with penitential hair.

I know your harsh ambition's iron creed,
Your engines dream, your datas chiming need;
You number futures, auction out the sun,
And bid the stubborn atom's labour run.
What temple's quiet could confine that tide?
Your will would drive a saint to break his pride.
And I—was I the convent or the blaze?
Sometimes your censor, oft your sweetest praise.
I played the abbess, rapt in sober rule,
Then threw the keys and bless'd the rebel school.

Forgive me, Heaven, I worshipp'd more than God
When first his letter kiss'd the linen clod;
Forgive me, soul, I drank the mortal bowl,
And found in mortal eyes my final goal.
If angels murmured, still I caught his breath,
And made my chapel in the courts of death.
All vows grew pale where fiercer incense rose—
His mind my censer, and his hands my close.
I sung of virtue—ah!—and shaped a lie,
For virtue starved when passion stood me by.

You call'd me "witch"—I wore the title fair,
A queen by night, by day a penitent's prayer;
I banned the sirens, yet became their sound,
And envied seas that kept your vessels bound.
I chided rides where drivers' glances fell,
And built a jealous watch around my cell.
O shameful courage! bold in petty things,
And faint when greater absolution sings.
If love be brave, why fights it in the dust,
And leaves the principal to small mistrust?

I know the story mortal women learn—
To hang a lamp where doubtful pathways turn;
To feign indifference, feed the hungry fire,
And scold the flame for burning where we hire.
Yet you, whose thought outpaces mortal time,
Would you be taught by my capricious crime?
I wish'd to rule, to kneel, to bless, to bind,
And flog the flesh to spare the luring mind.
I broke your heart to see if mine would mend;
It broke instead, and would not condescend.

The nights we swore a future's marble plan,
I wrote in air what stone could never span;
A table set with names and titles proud,
A dynasty inscribed within a cloud.
You smiled at heralds, coins, and laurel'd bays,
Then hurl'd their brass to fund your fiercer ways.
"My realm," you cried, "is matter's secret throne—
I'll crown the world with laws the world's not known."
O lawless heart that loved a law so vast—
I bless'd the vow, then trembled at the cast.

When Autumn smoulder'd in the college eaves,
And tongues made treaties under rustling leaves,
I saw you stand apart, a star in frost,
A general counting what the army cost.
Your gaze outran the room, the year, the slate,
And pinned a farther, crueler, brighter fate.
What place for rest within that marching mind?
What seat for me unless I kept behind?
Yet still I swore to pace your iron road,
And take the lash as equal to the load.

My friends grew pale, they whisper'd of my doom,
They saw a convent in my drawing-room;
They made me Reason's novice, dress'd in blame,
And wrote my sermon in another's name.
I read the page, and could not spell a line;
For every verse dissolv'd into your sign.
How learn the rule that bids a heart retreat,
When every retreat becomes your inward street?
O stern instructors! show me how to part—
They point the door, yet leave you in my heart.

Write, write me, Love—yet call me not your own;
The chain I choose is by myself alone.
I plead for freedom, beggars at the gate,
Then kiss the fetter and adore the weight.
If guilt be mine, I own its lovely hue;
No lily whiter than the blush of you.
But teach me too to honour noble pain,
And feel the worth of losses we maintain.
For still my better angel bites the rein,
And drags me from the precipice again.

O days when silence bound your lion tongue,
When lab and ledger claim'd you while I hung!
I measured hours by shadows on the floor,
And found at last a Sabbath without shore.
Then, cruel mercy! you return'd in flame,
And I forgot the sermon and my name.
What saint survives such visitations bright?
I hid my beads to greet the greater rite.
Your kiss absolved me of the vows I kept,
And I, absolved, to mortal longing wept.

Say, do you mock the trembling that you cause?
Are tyrants tender when they coin our laws?
Or do you, in your lonely empire stern,
Half-hate the hearts on which you seem to turn?
Confess—your solitude desires a queen,
But crowns her chiefly when she is not seen.
You call me near, then prize me most when far;
I am your altar's lamp and prison bar.
Thus both our cruelties in concord meet:
You wield the mind, and I provide the heat.

Yet is there hope that patience, school'd by pain,
May teach our vehement spirits to remain?
That strength once spent on jealous trifles low,
Might feed the work where nobler passions go?
That I may guard without the gaoler's chain,
And you may love without the world's disdain?
So might our storms, once lash'd at random shore,
Turn mills for bread and lanterns for the poor.
For who are we, if not two kindred fires,
Whose furies, yoked, can lift the dead desires?

I dream a cloister neither cold nor bright,
A house of labour lit by hallow'd light;
Where vows are made to truth, and not to pride,
And discipline is love made sanctified.
There I would stand—no jealous warder's eye—
A partner at the wheel that grinds the sky.
Not merely yours by fever's desperate creed,
But yours where courage learns the larger deed.
So shall the shrift of tears be seal'd in bread,
And grace descend where former folly bled.

Yet ah! the former folly will return;
One look, and ash forgets it once could burn.
I gird myself with sober thoughts and plain,
Then loose the knot to wear your chain again.
How oft I swear "No more"—the page still wet—
When "more" arrives with eyes I can't forget.
If Heaven would save me, let it save me now;
Or teach me how to keep the saner vow.
But if salvation means I lose your face,
Let angels spare me that protecting grace.

Perhaps, when years have school'd our tempers proud,
When fame has drunk her fill of cheering crowd,
We'll walk a quieter quay at eventide,
And watch the ships whose thunder once we'd ride.
Your talk will soften; I shall scarcely chide;
We'll laugh that jealousy became our guide.
Where ran our fear, a river clear shall run,
And bless the orchard ripen'd by the sun.
Then, turning back, we'll count the foolish scars,
And thank the wounds that taught us what we are.

But if that gentle morrow never be,
And storms forbid the harbour and the lee;
If all our vows are writ on whirling foam,
And every road returns me not to home;
Still let me say—though saints forbid the phrase—
I found my God in you in errant ways.
Not you the idol—no!—but God's fierce spark,
That breaks in men and sets alight the dark.
When I adored, I err'd in measure, true;
But error knelt before a hint of You.

Teach me to love the source beyond the sign,
To drink the spring, yet bless the guarding vine;
To lift the chalice without losing ground,
And, tasting sweetness, keep the higher sound.
So shall my zeal no more in frenzy live,
But learn from you the strength that can forgive.
I'll master envy as you master stars,
And let true pity uncreate my bars.
Then will I watch your battles, bless their end,
And loosely call you lover, firmly friend.

Forgive the times I made your labour bleed,
And bound your daylight with my private need;
Forgive the slights I polish'd into wrong,
The tests I set to prove your heart was strong.
Forgive the pride that would not let me lose,
That stabb'd with tears, then begg'd the wound to bruise.
Accept, instead, a humbler diadem—
A daily "yes" without the stratagem.
Not fiery oaths that fail at cock's first cry,
But bread and salt, that let the hunger die.

Say, if I come with calmer eyes at last,
Will you not love me better than the past?
Not for the drama but the faithful art,
That builds a room for two within one heart.
Your splendour needs no echo from my fears;
My beauty needs no mirror made of tears.
Let each stand whole, and each in wholeness meet—
Then kiss not armour, but the flesh made sweet.
So may our fiercest energies align,
And vow to bless what once they sought to mine.

Yet must I speak what trembling still remains—
I love the brink, the thunder in my veins.
Shall tame contentment cure so wild a taste,
Or pall the wine with water's careful haste?
O let me learn the higher, harder art:
To keep the flame, yet not consume the heart;
To want with honour, own desire's face,
And crown the want with freely chosen grace.
So when I ache, I'll know the ache is free,
No tyrant's chain—but hospitality.

You taught me ruthlessness against the lie,
To test, to cleave, to watch illusions die.
Teach me that tenderness is also true,
That greatness bends, but does not break, for you.
I'll teach you too—though imprecise my school—
That genius dwells in one more gentle rule:
The mind that conquers worlds will fail to reign,
Unless it learns to hold one heart from pain.
So shall our doctrines meet where wisdom grows:
Your law of force, my law of mercy's rose.

If I once shaped a dungeon out of love,
I now would open windows to the Dove;
Let jealous terrors fly like smoke at dawn,
And keep a watch that does not draw a yawn.
I'll trust the truth, not shadows on the wall,
And answer whispers with a brighter call.
No more the tally of imagined sin—
I'll count the goods, and call the goodness in.
Thus freed, I'm yours in freer, higher guise:
A comrade equal, not a pair of eyes.

Yet should you fail (for men like gods can fail),
Should tempests break the unforgiving sail;
Should harsh ambition grind what love has wrought,
And fame exact the usury of thought—
Still, I will mourn you without curse or spite,
And pray your morning through my deepest night.
For love that will not stoop to easy blame,
Has made of loss a sacramental flame.
Such loss refines the metal of the vow,
And prints the seal on wiser foreheads now.

I see a chapel neither stone nor law,
But built of habit, kindness, selfless awe;
There, softly, daily, shall we make our creed—
To tell the truth, to meet the other's need.
No organ swells, yet music fills the nave:
Your measured mind, the courage to be brave;
My singing heart, once shrill with startled pride,
Now finds the pitch where both our spirits ride.
The liturgy is bread and book and rest,
And peace the psalm that rises from the breast.

Tell me, my Love, if such a place can be,
Will you not walk its aisles and wait for me?
I'll bind my hair with ordinary ties,
And learn the art of looking with wise eyes.
No more a queen who mutinies at dawn,
But steward of the day that must be born.
Bring all your stars; I'll bring an earthly flame;
We'll light the table, bless each other's name.
And if our tempers rise as tempers will,
We'll lay them down, and know a grander still.

For still I fear—I am that mortal thing—
That loves too hard the emblem of a ring;
That covets seals and heralds' crimson art,
To compensate the tremor in the heart.
But while I fear, I also slowly learn:
The truest state is one we daily earn.
No grant of title steadies what we are—
The soul's ennobling is a hidden star.
Thus let the pageantry of youth go by;
I'll keep the truth that will not cheat nor die.

If once I made a shrine of my demand,
I now would build a hearth with open hand;
Where you may think and I may safely feel,
And none must prove, and none must beg or kneel.
So shall we live the doctrine tears have taught,
That love is brave, and jealousy is fraught;
That trust, through trials, harder gems reveals,
And laughter crowns what penitence conceals.
I'll hold your name without a chain of fear,
And you shall find your victories held dear.

If angels read the heart behind these lines,
Let them not write me debtor to my crimes;
Let mercy gloss where pride has scored too deep,
And charity correct what passions keep.
For though my tongue still stumbles on your praise,
My hands reach out to ordinary days;
To mend, to bake, to mark the times as sweet,
And fold your thunder with a gentler beat.
Where once I broke, I now would bravely bend,
And find in bending not an end, but mend.

Take then this scroll, unfinish'd though it seems,
A river-charm between our wilder streams.
Not final law, but honest early word,
The first clear bell by which new hours are heard.
If you can love me walking, not on fire,
I'll love you thinking, not on steel desire.
And if the blaze returns—as oft it must—
We'll feed it truth, and starve its petty rust.
So shall our ancient battle change its name,
And, call'd by love, still keep its lion frame.

Whether we meet in cloister'd autumn light,
Or part in rooms made colder by the night,
Believe me this: the storms that made me wild
Have taught me now to cherish mercy mild.
And if I fail (as habits fondly do),
I'll rise and try the better way to you.
For where you go—through calculus or cloud—
I keep a lamp, unseen, yet burning proud.
Not watchman's fear, nor jealous sentinel,
But faithful fire that learns at last to dwell.

And when our story bends to older years,
And quiet halls exchange the younger cheers,
We'll find within the wreckage of our youth
A rarer wine extracted by the truth.
You'll read my faults as footnotes to your page,
I'll mark your iron tempered into sage.
What once was wound becomes a holy mark,
A testament to walking through the dark.
Thus hand in hand, not conqueror and slave,
But fellow pilgrims braver for the grave.

Farewell?—No, never: fare-well means "fare thus,"
To fare in ways more worthy both of us.
So hear me, Love: I place my crown below,
And lift a simpler garland still to grow.
If I am queen, I'll reign in common days;
If I am witch, I'll charm in wholesome ways.
If I am nun, I'll pray with open door,
And make of prayer the task to love you more.
But chiefly this: I'll choose, and choose again,
The path where heart and conscience aren't at war.

Thus writes the hand once quick to break and bind,
Now slower, steadier, schooling heart and mind.
Receive the seal, not of a jealous past,
But of a love that means to learn at last.
And if it please the heavens, be it so—
Elizabeth—your penitent—your foe—your Beau.

My dear—if I may still presume that name—
How shall I write and not ignite the flame?
You choose new altars, teach another's grace,
Yet keep my measurements in every place.
You fit her dresses cut to suit my frame,
You whisper fabrics, scents, and brands I'd name;
You train her gait, the posture of the wrist,
And tutor taste the way I once insist.
She takes the chair where I had placed my vow,
And learns, by you, the arts I live in now.

You said you never want my blessing, no—
If we are sunder'd, happiness is foe.
You want me sick with knowledge of your joy,
You want regret to be your chosen ploy.
You swore a future: younger, slimmer, taught,
A pupil in the wardrobe I had wrought;
You promised she would carry what I lack,
And walk the life you meant for me to track.
Your message stung—its candour was a knife—
To see a stranger dress in my old life.

You placed your perfumes side by side—how bold—
Bleu, Mademoiselle, martinis going cold;
You took her hand, and, smiling at the proof,
Hung out our quarrel from the towered roof.
I answered "rude"—a thin and tardy shield—
The blow was given long before I yield.
You wanted me to bruise, to bite, to break,
To feel an ache you swore you would not fake;
And, God forgive me, part of me agreed—
For once I'd taught you taste, you taught me need.

You write: you begged me love the man, not face;
That I chose friends' disdain above your grace.
You say I told you, "Find a gaze that sees,"
And now I rage you found such eyes with ease.
If truth is shard and not a single glass,
Then I must hold the shard I cannot pass:
I, too, mistook the mirror for the man,
And scorn'd the raw for lack of proper plan.
Forgive me, if you can, the cruel art
That keeps a flawless standard, not a heart.

You say I fashion'd you and sent you forth—
A groom'd Achilles, armour'd for the North;
That I refined your cuff, your collar's line,
And taught your senses how to read a sign;
That now another reaps the careful field
I sow'd with patience, then refused to yield.
I hear it; in the ledger of our wrong,
That entry stands, exact, severe, and long.
Yet credit, too, the coin you paid to me:
Your iron gave my wavering courage fee.

You prophesied a scene to salt the years:
A Napa lobby, glass with husband's cheers;
Your wife beside you, younger, bright, and trim,
A child in tow, your firm at flood-tide brim;
And I at thirty-five, with empty ring,
Would meet your triumph like a chastening.
You swore I'd reach to cancel choices made,
And find the page already pressed and laid.
Why sketch my future in that flinty chalk,
Then set your foot upon my narrow walk?

You teach her shoes that never touch'd her feet,
You fit the last to arches mine had meet;
You tell her why the kid must crease this way,
Why patent speaks at dusk but not at day;
You show her coats for autumn's Oxford gloam,
And where to sit in Union's lamplit dome.
She learns our places: Sapp's, the bodega's light,
The campus paths for Prufrock's halting night;
You take her where you swore you meant for me—
And call it providence, or therapy.

You wrote: you'd slow your thunder, curb your haste,
Be less intense, let daily mercies taste;
You set before me terms that sound like grace,
Then placed her in my consecrated space.
You even left a door that was not shut—
A covenant if I would make the cut:
"A vow within six months," you said, "be wife,
Or else she lives the architecture's life."
I heard the scaffold knocking in the wind,
And knew the law would bind what love had thinn'd.

You say the Corsican will tire and go,
That I shall grind him down as others so;
That rage will be my craft, and frost my trade,
And mercy shutter'd like a room I made.
Perhaps—my temper is a clattering gate,
My jealous mind a mason's piling slate;
But while you balance prophecies and pain,
Remember: you have chisell'd much of mine.
We built each other—true; but at the last,
We weaponised what made us whole in past.

You posted proof; I practise being calm—
I write, I breathe, I sort the balm from harm.
You carved me out—"tetelestai"—you said,
As surgeons chant when stitching up the red.
You spoke to Mother; she pronounced it right,
You wrote an elegy she thought polite.
Then fury rose, and grief unbound her hair,
And I—O God—was heedless, sharp, unfair.
I spoke like weather breaking over stone,
And left you bleeding in a room alone.

You say I yammer money like a bell,
That every talk I bent to that one well;
That in the ledger where our kindness lives,
I charged you tolls no tender spirit gives.
If gold profaned our language into trade,
Then I will own the profanation made.
I wanted proof that would not ebb or run—
A sort of contract swearing you were one;
But love that clings to clauses, stamps, and seals
Becomes a deed that neither signer feels.

You claim you cut to save yourself—and bled;
Antler withdrew, your pitch went thin with dread;
You ask how you will fund the child you keep,
The girl who is not I, the dream in sleep.
I will not gloat; I will not mark the score;
I know the math of ruin to the core.
The cost is ours—of fury, pride, and pose—
Of staging life as theatre to our woes.
I will not say "be happy"; I refuse
That tidy blessing you forbid to use.

Yet know the sight of her in what was mine
Is less a pang of envy than a sign:
You clothe a pilgrim for a route we drew,
You feed a guest with bread I learn'd to brew;
You coach a smile to fit the frame I wore,
And teach her how to pass through my old door.
It is not she I grieve, but sacrament—
A marriage of our meanings, now mis-spent.
I mourn the house we schemed—its hearth and light—
And watch you furnish it for someone bright.

I'm not the saint you want me to become,
Nor merely sinner beating on a drum;
I am a woman, proud, incisive, poor,
Whose strength becomes a lock, whose love a door.
I taught you fabrics; you taught me the stars;
We both mistook our gifts for rightful bars.
We tempered each the other to a blade,
Then tried that edge on all the oaths we made.
If now you forge a different sword to lift,
Remember who first hammered out the gift.

Do not instruct me how to meet you, years
And Napa later, varnish'd with my tears.
If fate should stage that lobby's glassy glow,
And you, with child and wife, move sweet and slow,
I will not beg to barter time's decree,
Nor scratch at doors that never open'd me.
But let it not be triumph in your throat—
Remember all the lines I help'd you note:
How cuffs should break, what notes to tip the room,
How not to wear a victory like doom.

You say you want my sorrow as your meat,
My jealousy to sugar all you eat;
And I—who once demanded proofs from you—
Am tempted still to give you what is due.
But no: the lesson learnt in later days
Is not to feed a hunger born of praise.
I will not mark your steps with salt and ash,
Nor launch a curse that boomerangs to lash.
I set my pen to name what love has cost,
And then I fold the ledger for the lost.

You loved my scent, the massed and minor art,
The ritual that armours up the heart;
You say you'll train her—till she walks as I,
Till even silence sounds the same reply.
Teach her, then—how to leave a room with poise,
How not to wear an ankle like a noise;
Show her that satin bruises before dawn,
That pearl needs breath, and leather must be worn.
But tell her, too, the thing you would not hear:
That discipline is gentleness made clear.

I have a list of places meant for us—
The corner table, booth with private fuss;
The autumn bench where Prufrock finds his fear,
The bodega where the neon hums like prayer;
The sushi plate where you would order mine,
The quiet street that smelt of clove and pine.
These are not trophies I can stand to see
Paraded in another's mimicry.
They are not jealous claims to guard or own—
They are the roads I learn to walk alone.

If I am cruel, it's in a sharpening way:
I draw a line so love can learn to stay.
You asked for vows; I offer not a ring,
But mercy, which is fiercer than a thing.
I bless the woman taught to wear my shoe,
I bless the child that may look back at you;
I bless the evenings you will not recall,
The talks that dull the blade and spare the fall.
And then I bless myself—to end the fight,
To choose a decent ordinary light.

Yet hear me: should you wish to lay aside
The little wars that make a larger pride;
Should you prefer a craft to best a mood,
And build a life by labour into good;
Should you recall that bread and book and rest
Once seem'd a psalm that rose within your chest—
Know there was once a house that bore our sign,
Whose lintel I anointed, yours and mine.
Its door stands open inward to the past;
I will not dwell there, but I've shut it last.

You taught me, too, that pity must be steel,
That grandeur bends so tenderness can heal.
So take this letter—both a thought and thread—
A bandage laid across what once we bled.
I will not ask if she can run in heels,
Or raise an eyebrow at her ways and meals;
I won't compare the timbre of her voice,
Or weigh her manners like a counterpoise.
I only ask you guard one vow we knew:
Do not make theatre of the good and true.

I end as I began—in second person, "you,"
Because we only ever loved by two.
But I will learn a grammar less possess'd,
Where verbs are gentle, and the nouns can rest.
May all the wardrobes open without pain;
May shoes be worn for walking, not for reign;
May restaurants remember what we meant,
But not become a court of punishment.
And may the life you meant for me to wear
Become, in time, a life I will not bear.

If I must see her in my tailored day,
I'll let the sight be schooling, not dismay.
For cloth can be remade, and meaning move;
For taste can grow, and mercy prove its love.
What's left for me is neither curse nor plea—
It's choosing what I'll keep of you in me.
Not Napa's theatre, not the cruel parade,
But quiet skill and courage unafraid.
I fold those up, like linens, washed and new,
And put them in a drawer that locks from you.
