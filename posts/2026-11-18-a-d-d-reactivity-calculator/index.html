A D-D reactivity calculator

How often deuterium fuses, at any temperature, in your browser

Published 2026-11-18 · Short · Tags fusion, plasma physics, tools

Laurelin burns deuterium alone. Two deuterons fuse along two branches with almost equal odds: one makes a triton and a proton, the other makes helium-3 and a 2.45 MeV neutron. The rate of both depends steeply on temperature, through the thermal reactivity ⟨σv⟩.

This calculator uses the Bosch and Hale parametrization (Nuclear Fusion 32, 611, 1992), the standard fit, valid from 0.5 to 190 keV. The code is a port of the module we use at Laurelin to check every reactivity number, and it agrees with that module to better than one part in a million. Set the ion temperature and the deuterium density.








Two things to see. The reactivity rises by about five orders of
magnitude between 1 and 50 keV, which is why D-D needs a hotter plasma
than D-T. And the branch fractions move with temperature: the neutron
branch carries a little more than half of the reactions, and its share
grows as the plasma gets hotter. A yield estimate has to use the
fraction at the operating temperature.


The power figure counts only the primary reactions. The tritium and
helium-3 they make can burn again, which this page leaves out.
