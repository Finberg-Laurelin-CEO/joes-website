---
title: Time is not entropy
subtitle: Notes on "Remarks on the Fundamentals of Time"
date: 2025-08-19

---

Physicists often say that time and entropy are the same thing. The second law gives us an arrow: entropy goes up, so the past is different from the future. From there it is a short step to the claim that time *is* the growth of entropy.

In [Remarks on the Fundamentals of Time](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.14312) I argue that this step is a category error. Entropy explains the *direction* of time. Time itself needs a separate explanation.

The test case is heat death. Take a universe at maximum entropy. Nothing gets more disordered, so the arrow is gone. But temporal structure is still there. The correlations of the quantum vacuum persist, and so does the geometry of spacetime. Both are temporal facts, and neither needs an entropy gradient.

So the end state is a twilight of time, and the subtitle of the paper, *die Zeitdämmerung*, comes from that picture. Time keeps its structure and loses its direction.

This matters for more than cosmology. If time and entropy are separate, then a theory of time has to name what grounds it beyond the second law. My candidates are the vacuum correlations and the spacetime geometry. The paper makes that case in more detail.

The same word gives the subtitle of my novel, *False Vacuum: Die Zeitdämmerung*, which takes the idea less seriously.
